# Perf microtest ownership correction: expert source proposal

Functional McKernel OS/apps and Rust/assembly migration only; no security testing.

Status: DESIGN ONLY. No runner, compiler, fixture, fault-injection test, native or guard was executed. No runtime release or profile admission follows from this document. This does not change either prior runner or its findings. A new exact implementation, independent review, fresh admission and reviewed operation controls are required before use.

## Why the two candidates fail

The original serial runner can block before a timeout or lose ownership during launch. Its successor puts an internally monitored worker in its own process group, but can let it work before its monitor publishes a confirmed identity; compiler leader wait does not establish descendant retirement; and an outer watchdog directed only at the supervisor cannot retire the separately grouped worker. Its pure decision tests do not establish these properties.

## Narrow correction to implement and review

Use a single process group for the complete test family, with a separate external owner outside that group. Do not use the successor's separate worker process group. The external owner starts one controlled group leader and retains it as an unreaped direct child until all group retirement decisions are finished. The leader starts in a gated state and creates no descendants and does no staging until the external owner has established and recorded `(pid, start_ticks, pgid=pid, session)` from live procfs, confirmed that it is still the unreaped child, and explicitly sends the one-shot GO over a private inherited pipe. EOF, malformed message, signal, identity mismatch, record failure or a deadline before GO means no work; preserve UNKNOWN where ownership is not positively established. Set the group in the forked child before waiting for GO, with cancellation blocked around fork/publication; the owner must also handle child death before or during handshake. Do not infer group ownership from an expected PID alone.

The external owner stays runnable and supervises the one group during all staging, process creation, compilation, fixture execution and output recording. It applies the parent deadline and up to five seconds of group-only termination. It checks the unreaped leader's birth and PGID before each group signal; the leader remains unreaped as the group anchor until the group's complete census is absent except for the leader, including zombies. If procfs, identity, signal, or retirement proof fails, record UNKNOWN, retain evidence and do not claim cleanup. The owner's monitor must be independently inspected as the actual external watchdog and covered by its caller's established supervision; a statement that such a caller exists is not evidence of its implementation. If an independent external watchdog cannot reliably retain/control the same group, this design remains blocked. Do not claim a watchdog on the basis of GNU timeout's hash alone; its exact invocation, group creation, signal semantics and caller ownership must first be demonstrated under review.

The single group leader serially starts the exact approved compiler and fixture. After the compiler leader exits, observe it with `waitid(WNOWAIT)` and keep it unreaped while checking the complete owned group. The fixture is permitted only after positive compiler exit status and the absence of every other group member except the leader and the exited compiler; a surviving descendant, including an unreaped zombie, is not retirement. Reap the compiler only after that condition and verify its wait status. Unexpected or unobservable descendants, a failed census, or deadline means stop with no fixture. The external owner continues monitoring the entire interval. The same all-descendant rule applies to fixture retirement before success. This uses the reviewed compiler's trusted non-escaping process behavior, not an assertion that process groups contain adversarial processes; verify the exact compiler/helper behavior and profile assumptions in the execution review. A group scan alone does not prove that a child did not leave the group.

## Required fault-injection checks before release

Under a separately admitted cheap test profile, use inert test processes, private output roots and no compiler or fixture. Check at least: (1) owner fails before identity publication or closes its GO pipe, and the child never records action; (2) owner withholds GO and times out, then observes complete group retirement; (3) identity mismatch or procfs read error blocks GO and causes UNKNOWN; (4) fake compiler exits successfully while a grandchild remains alive, and fixture is never started; (5) compiler descendant remains zombie/unobservable, and retirement stays UNKNOWN; (6) supervisor blocks before and during child launch, while the external watchdog terminates the same owned group within the allowed cleanup interval; (7) signal during fork/publication cannot authorize an unowned group; (8) owner sees unexpected child or changed anchor and refrains from signaling an unverified group; (9) positive serial completion retains actual command, status and group-retirement evidence. Inject errors into the actual implementation and assert artifacts/absence of fixture, rather than testing only duplicated decision logic. Establish resource accounting, process-count handling, sanitised environment, pinned tool identities and custody before these tests.

## Existing runner applicability

The reviewed cheap profile names Python test entrypoints and shell wrappers; its direct-Rust extension admits only the bound perf fixture and explicitly requires inspection of the process-owning runner. Neither independently establishes a reusable implementation of the required external owner, handshake and family retirement. The already reviewed container/native owners govern different physical operations and cannot be assumed to authorize this unprivileged microtest. Therefore do not repurpose either without a scope-matched source and execution review.
